## Session 3 — 6:35 AM: The graph, and why the daily note was the hub

Aly sent a screenshot of the Obsidian graph with `2026-09-18` as the biggest node dead in the middle, and said: *"It should be inbox and tsa !personal."* That reframed the question he had asked twice — **"the vault starts from 18/9 notes"** was never about folder order. It was about what the graph opens on.

### The measurement, before touching anything

| Node | Out | In | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2026-09-18` | **30** | **1** | 31 |
| `VAULT-INDEX` | 15 | 1 | 16 |
| `Inbox` | 0 | 1 | 1 |

**A hub is inbound-heavy. That daily note was a firehose** — 202 wikilinks in one file, pointing at almost every note in the vault, with nothing pointing back. Obsidian's force layout puts the most-connected node in the middle, so a frozen log was the centre of the vault and the root index was on the rim.

### The cause, and it was already half-fixed by accident

Sessions 1 to 26 wrote "Notes Touched" as `[[wikilinks]]`. Sessions 27 and 28 wrote them as backticked paths, and so did the 19th. **Nobody wrote the rule down, so the switch never propagated backwards and nobody knew there had been a switch.**

### What changed

1. **Daily notes never wikilink.** 251 wikilinks across the three daily notes converted to backticks. They reference notes and files by name now. **Inbound links stay** — `Daily Notes.md` links to each one, which is what fixed their orphan problem in Session 2 this morning. The two fixes are opposite directions of the same rule: **a log is linked to, never from.**
2. **Every folder index now ends with an `**Up:**` line** back to `VAULT-INDEX`, plus its parent where it has one. That took the root index from 1 inbound to 10 and turned it from a spoke into a hub.
3. **`Inbox` rewritten as the front door it was supposed to be.** It was a 1-edge stub with no outbound links — a dot on the rim. It now carries the routing table: what lands here, where each kind of thing goes, the rule that a move updates both indexes, and what deliberately does *not* come through (daily notes, and anything with an obvious home). 10 outbound edges.
4. **Daily notes are `project: tsa`, not `personal`.** The mapping rule said `01 - Daily Notes/* -> personal` unconditionally, which contradicted the "content wins" line directly above it in the same section. These days are almost entirely agency work. A genuinely personal day still takes `personal`.

### The result

| Node | Out | In | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Alex Foods` | 11 | **19** | 30 |
| `VAULT-INDEX` | 15 | **10** | 25 |
| `Active Priorities` | 16 | 7 | 23 |
| `Inbox` | 10 | 1 | 11 |
| `2026-09-18` | **0** | **1** | 1 |

**The live client is the biggest node and the root index is second.** That is what a working vault should look like: the graph shows the shape of the work, not the shape of the logging.

### Decisions Made

- **A log is linked to, never from.** Written into `CLAUDE.md` and `VAULT-INDEX.md` so no future session re-breaks it. This is the same failure Session 7 on 18 Sep diagnosed and fixed for folder indexes, reappearing from the other direction — that fix added inbound links to the indexes and never noticed the log was still spraying outbound links at everything.
- **Edited append-only daily notes, deliberately.** The append-only rule protects the *record* — what happened, in order, never overwritten. Changing `[[X]]` to `` `X` `` changes no fact in the log. Every word is intact.
- **`Up:` lines rather than a second hub line.** Reciprocal linking is what makes an index inbound-heavy; another list of outbound links in the root would have made the problem worse.

### What I got wrong in this session

Ran a ghost-node check that did not strip inline code spans, so it reported five phantom wikilinks (`` `[[links]]` ``, `` `[[old name]]` `` and three more) as broken graph nodes. **They were already inside backticks and Obsidian never rendered them as links.** Nothing was wrong and nothing needed fixing. The checker was wrong, not the vault — the same lesson banked in `design-system/README.md`: verify the check before rewriting the thing.
