# Content Plan — Alex Foods, the three-month term

The strategy and the calendar behind [[Alex Foods]]' Package A: what gets posted, why, in what order across the three paid months, and what the numbers have to say by month 3. Serves **Foundation deliverable 5** and governs the monthly calendar Package A owes every month.

Built on [[Marketing]] — read `jareds-takes.md` and `marketing-content.md` before changing anything here. Brand facts in [[Alex Foods Brands]], tone in [[Brand Voice Guide]], campaigns in [[Giveaway Programme]], production in [[Package A Month 1]].

---

## 1. Translating the playbook, because it was not written for this

[[Marketing]] comes from a direct-response information business: content → lead magnet → tripwire → core offer, all online, all measurable, all owned. **Alex Foods sells sachets in shops in Alexandria.** There is no landing page, no email list, no checkout. Applying the funnel literally produces nonsense.

**What carries, unchanged, and it is most of it:**

- **You are not selling the product, you are selling the feeling.** The four feelings are already worked out in [[Slogans and Song]] §3 and they drive every post here.
- **Help them, do not talk about yourself.** *"Company X has been in business for X years"* is named in the playbook as the thing nobody cares about. **Alex Foods' own legacy creative does exactly that** — «جودة نثق بها منذ 20 عامًا». It is the trap this brand is already standing in.
- **Always point to the next step.** Non-negotiable, and §2 is what the next step actually is.
- **Content is an ungated lead magnet.**
- **Know exactly who you are talking to.** Four ranges, four different people, already mapped in [[Brand Voice Guide]] §3–§6.
- **Turn numbers into verdicts**, and **judge against a baseline** — §7.

**What does not carry:**

- **Tripwire and core offer.** There is no ascending price ladder. A 2 EGP sachet *is* the product.
- **Cost per opt-in, cost per view as the primary score.** Those are paid-media numbers and they belong to campaign management, not to organic content.
- **"Write long, build it for skimmers."** That is sales-page advice. A caption is four lines.

## 2. The funnel Alex Foods actually has — and the asset nobody has named

Work backwards, as the playbook says. The chain is short and it is physical:

> **See the post → recognise the pack in the shop → buy it → turn it over → scan the QR → enter the giveaway.**

**Every post's next step is the same and it is not "link in bio." It is *turn the packet over*.** That is an unusual CTA and it is the whole architecture. A post that does not eventually put a hand on a pack is a dead end.

### The thing that matters more than any of this

**"Own your audience. Don't build on rented land."** Followers on Meta are rented — the playbook is blunt about it, and one account problem takes the lot overnight.

**The QR scan is the only mechanism in this entire business that turns a stranger into a contact Alex Foods owns.** Not the page. Not the followers. The scan.

So the genuinely valuable outcome of three months is **not** an engagement rate. It is **how many real people are in the database behind that QR code**, and whether anyone can reach them again.

> **And right now nobody on TSA's side knows where that QR points**, which means the single most valuable asset the campaign could build may be getting thrown away every time somebody scans. [[Giveaway Programme]] §9, still unanswered. **This is the highest-value unanswered question on the account** and it is worth more than any amount of content planning.

**It does not replace the engagement goal.** Engagement is the leading indicator — it is what proves the content is working, monthly, while the list builds underneath. Both get measured. §7.

## 3. The supply problem, found by putting two pages of the shipped kit next to each other

The Brand and Social Kit specifies a posting rhythm. The contract specifies a production quantity. **They have never been checked against each other.**

| | Per week | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram, Reels + posts | 5 | ~22 |
| Instagram Stories | daily | ~30 |
| Facebook | 4 | ~17 |
| TikTok | 3–4 | ~15 |
| **Total slots** | **~19** | **~84** |

**The contract produces 20 original assets a month.** 12 graphics, 6 animated stills, 2 animations.

Take Stories out — they are cheap, repurposed, and mostly polls, questions and reposts. That leaves **~54 feed and Reel slots against 20 produced assets.**

> **It only closes if every single asset cross-posts to all three platforms, roughly 2.7 times each, with no platform-native variation at all.** It is achievable and it has **zero slack**. It also quietly means Facebook — the platform the kit itself weights up because that is where the mothers are — receives Instagram content verbatim.

**Three things make it work rather than break:**

1. **Stories carry the daily load and are not produced assets.** Polls, questions, reposts, behind-the-scenes, repeat of the day's feed post. Cheap on purpose.
2. **The client's winner photos and videos are free content, and they are the slack in this plan.** They already exist, more are coming, and they are the most credible thing the account can post. **Nothing else fills a daily story slot for three months.**
3. **TikTok takes the Reels verbatim.** Already the kit's own rule.

**If it still does not close, the rhythm gets cut, not the quality.** A page posting 3 good things a week beats a page posting 5 thin ones, and the contract quantity is fixed at 20.

## 4. The three months

Month 1 starts at written Foundation sign-off. On the current target that is around **9 October**, so the term runs roughly **October, November, December 2026** — verify against the real sign-off date before anything is scheduled.

| | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campaign** | **School gifts** — the client's own #1 | **Bike, scooter, balls** — resumes | **Umrah** — comes off the shelf |
| **Job** | Launch the presence. **Teach the mechanic.** | **Breadth.** Prove four ranges, one company | **Convert.** Turn an audience into a list |
| **Hero** | **BeBo**, by the client's instruction | BeBo leads, AlRawy rises | Alex Foods itself |
| **Season** | School year opens. Still warm | Lunchbox season. Cold arrives | Winter. **2MAN weights down** |
| **Primary number** | **Establish the baseline** | Beat month 1 | **The goal, §7** |

**The seasonal read, and it is not decoration.** 2MAN is ice pops. Alexandria in December is not ice-pop weather, and pushing a summer SKU into winter to keep a rotation even is how a content plan starts lying about the business. **AlRawy is the winter range** — nectar in a lunchbox, school in full swing, the buyer's "I did not cut a corner" feeling is at its strongest exactly when the mornings get cold. BeBo and POLEKA are year-round.

**Month 3 carries a tone problem that has to be solved in month 2, not month 3.** Umrah cannot be advertised in the voice that sells jelly sweets. Quiet, plain, respectful, no characters, no exclamation marks. Decide it while it is parked and cheap. [[Slogans and Song]] §5.

## 6. Months 2 and 3 — structure, not scripts

**Deliberately not written out post by post, and that is the contract's own rule:** the calendar is delivered before the month it covers begins. Writing December's captions in September produces content that ignores everything months 1 and 2 teach — and month 1's whole job is to produce a baseline that changes what month 2 should be.

**Month 2 — breadth.** The bike-and-scooter giveaway resumes, so the mechanic is already taught and the creative can spend its energy on the ranges instead. AlRawy rises with the lunchbox season. POLEKA's characters carry the 30% character pillar. Heavier on winner content, because by then there is a pile of it. **The read from month 1 decides the weighting** — whichever pillar carried engagement gets more room.

**Month 3 — convert.** Umrah in its own register. Alex Foods itself steps forward as the master brand rather than a badge on a range. **And the month the numbers are answered for.** §7.

## 7. The engagement goal, and the honest problem with setting one today

He asked for an engagement goal at month 3. **Here is the goal, and here is why the number is not invented in this note.**

### The metric is engagement rate, not followers

Followers are the vanity number. **Engagement rate — interactions divided by reach, per post** — is the one that says whether the content is any good, and it stays comparable whether the page has 500 followers or 50,000.

### The problem: there is no baseline, and inventing one is worse than admitting it

The playbook is blunt about this: *"If you're just starting out and have nothing to compare against, then you run some ads and send some emails and see what comes back, because that's the only way to get a baseline in the first place."*

**We do not even know whether Alex Foods has existing pages**, with followers and history, or whether this starts from zero — that question is open in [[Package A Month 1]] §5 and it changes the answer completely. **Quoting an industry benchmark from memory would be exactly the invented precision this vault exists to prevent.** When the pages exist, the benchmark comes from Meta's own reporting, not from me.

### So the goal is set as a multiple, and it is set at the end of month 1

> **Month 3 goal: engagement rate at or above 1.5× the month-1 baseline, measured on a rolling 28 days, with the audience at least doubled over the same period.**
>
> **The baseline is week 2 to week 4 of month 1** — week 1 is launch noise and it always flatters.

1.5× is deliberate. Content that is working improves as the audience sharpens and the weak pillars get cut. **Anything less than 1.5× over two months means the format is fine but not learning**, and a plan that does not learn is a plan that just repeats.

### The verdicts, decided now so nobody has to have an opinion later

The playbook again: *decide the thresholds ahead of time and let the number make the call.*

| Month-3 reading | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **≥ 1.5× baseline** | **Scale.** Put media spend behind the winning pillar and take the format into the renewal conversation |
| **1.0× – 1.5×** | **Hold and cut.** Content works, the mix does not. Drop the weakest pillar, give its slots to the strongest |
| **< 1.0×** | **Rebuild the format.** Not the brand, not the voice — the format. Something about how it is being posted is wrong |

**And the number that decides renewal is not on this table.** It is the QR list, §2. A contract with no auto-renewal gets renewed on evidence the business grew, and *"engagement rate rose 1.5×"* is a much weaker sentence than *"you now have a database of your own customers."*

### Three things to measure that are not engagement

1. **Scans.** The actual outcome. **Currently invisible** — Meta cannot see a scan on a pack in a shop, so unless the destination is instrumented this cannot be reported at all. [[Giveaway Programme]] §3.
2. **Saves and shares** over likes. On a giveaway a share is someone recruiting for you.
3. **"How do I enter" comments, falling over time.** If that question keeps coming, the mechanic is not landing and A2 has failed. **A falling question count is content working.**

> **And the standing warning from the analytics playbook: read every number through context before reacting.** A rolling window, never a single day. If a number goes flat or reads zero, check the tracking before checking the strategy.

## 8. What this plan needs before it is real

1. **Where the QR points.** §2. Highest-value unanswered question on the account.
2. **Do they already have pages, with followers and history?** Decides whether the baseline is a measurement or a starting gun.
3. **The school campaign's actual dates.** G12 is a deadline post with no deadline.
4. **The gifts mockups.** S6 and G2 are the prize, and no artwork for it exists.
5. **Winner photos and videos, with written permission to publish.** §3 depends on them for the daily slot, and consent to receive a prize is not consent to appear in an ad.
6. **Meta admin.** TSA posts. Without it none of this is publishable.
7. **Aly's ear on every Arabic line above**, and on Alex Foods' own master tone, which is still the one unwritten block in [[Brand Voice Guide]].
