### The fourth defect, and it is the same defect as the other three

`.tyl p` has specificity (0,1,1). `.d1` has (0,1,0). So **a 21pt display statement rendered at 7.2pt** and looked like nothing more than a timid design choice. I set it to 18pt, then 21pt, and the render did not change — because the rule was never winning.

Traps 4, 5 and 7 in the README are now recorded as **one trap wearing three coats: a CSS rule silently beating the one you meant.** A descendant `display:block`, a reused class name, a specificity override. **None of them error. None of them look broken in a screenshot.** The only thing that finds them is walking the DOM and reading `getComputedStyle` against what you intended. That method has now caught four real defects in this build that repeated passes of looking at PNGs missed every time.

### What I got wrong, twice, and what it cost

**Reading one:** treated "it should look like this" as a request to restyle the Design System, and spent a turn arguing against it on the basis of a locked decision. The decision was real, but it was not what he was asking about.

**Reading two:** built the kit with TSA's visual devices and my own content — contrast verdicts stamped on frames, ΔE figures, brand-architecture theory. **Governance information in a kit's clothing.** A kit is for somebody who has to make a post on Tuesday, not for somebody adjudicating whether a post is compliant.

**The lesson worth keeping: when a reference document is handed over, the information architecture is part of the reference, not just the styling.** "Make it look like this" means the sections too.

- **`#05004B` survives as the hero-panel and sign-off colour**, so the master is still present on a light page.
- **Red stays display-only.** 4.41:1 on Paper clears 3:1 and misses 4.5:1, so the red panel carries display type and its footnote sits below it on the ground. The kit does not break its own rule on its own page.

### The BeBo conflict, and I went to the artwork rather than picking a side

**The client says BeBo is "juice in a sachet you drink directly from with a straw."** The vault has said since 18 September that BeBo is **powder, one sachet makes a jug** — and the entire voice section rests on it: the feeling is *permission*, the words-in list is ظرف and إبريق, the lead caption is «ظرف واحد يعمل إبريق كامل».

So I opened their own packs and looked properly, at the source mockup rather than the thumbnails.

**What is actually printed:** a **pillow bag**, not a hand-sized sachet. «مشروب بطعم الخوخ / Peach Drink». The peach pack carries **a cup with a straw standing in it**; the cola pack carries **a tall glass of cola over ice**. Both are pictures of a drink already poured. **And no BeBo pack carries «أدخل الماصة هنا» — AlRawy prints it across the top of every single pouch.** That print is the difference between the two ranges and it is on one of them only.

**Most likely the person answering described AlRawy and applied it to both.** The two answers are near-identical sentences.

**But I am not letting TSA off either.** The word *powder* appears nowhere on the front of the pack, and neither does a jug. **"One sachet makes a jug" is our inference**, and a pillow bag could perfectly well hold a strip of individual sachets, which would make both accounts true at once. So the honest state is that **both readings are unsupported**, and one photo of the back of a pack ends it.

**BeBo copy is frozen until that photo arrives** — and BeBo is now, by their own answer, the **priority range and the best seller**. Worst possible range to be wrong about.

### The one that is a legal problem, not a factual one

**They called AlRawy "organic juice."** Two separate issues and both land on TSA: the pack says **nectar**, and **organic is a certified claim rather than a describing word.** It is a bigger exposure than «طبيعي» because it asserts a certification nobody has seen, and **TSA runs the paid campaigns** — so an ad carrying it is TSA's ad account under review.

Banned in every language and on every surface until a certificate exists. **And the right move is to ask for it properly rather than only refuse the word** — if they hold it, it is a genuine asset and it changes AlRawy's positioning completely.

### The numbers, which are the most useful thing in the whole return

| Platform | Followers |
|---|---|
| Facebook | **~52,000** |
| TikTok | ~500 |
| Instagram | ~40 |

Four things fall straight out of those three numbers:

1. **The umbrella decision is confirmed by real data.** The accounts are `alex.foods` — **one presence carrying everything, not four brand pages.** The architecture we chose on 18 September is the architecture they already run.
2. **The kit's Facebook weighting stops being reasoning and becomes measurement.** 52,000 against 540 across both other platforms combined.
3. **"All platforms" is not the real answer.** Instagram at 40 and TikTok at 500 are placeholders. **One asset exists.**
4. **And the tension nobody had named: the 52,000 are almost certainly not kids.** They say the buyers are mainly kids. Kids are on TikTok, where they have 500 followers. **The audience they have and the audience they describe are on two different platforms.**

**The call I made rather than hedged:** month 1 works the 52,000, because it is the only place the contracted KPI can move inside a month and the parent audience is right for BeBo-as-school-drink. TikTok gets built in parallel on the character content — the faces, the animals and the running boy are **already drawn and already paid for**, and they are native to the platform the kids are on. Instagram gets cross-posts and nothing else until it earns attention. Spreading evenly across three platforms from these baselines would spend the retainer proving nothing.

### Decisions Made

- **Company age settled at 20, not 25.** Their answer was "20 at least." Copy uses «أكتر من ٢٠ سنة» and never 25, and their own 25 YEARS badge is inconsistent with their own seal — worth telling them.
- **The goal conflict gets bridged rather than escalated.** They want engagement *and* "mostly more shops," which are different outcomes. **For a factory, an engaged page is a trade argument** — a distributor is reassured by visible demand. So the engagement number is the contracted KPI, the shop count is the business case, the report carries both, and **the only content change is repointing the 10% company slice at the trade.** Cheapest way to serve both answers and it stays inside scope.
- **Three approvers is a contract problem and gets closed to one.** They named Mohamed and Hussein then a manager. **Clause 3 allows one**, and three means a delivery approved by one gets re-opened by another. Mohamed as day-to-day contact underneath one named approver works fine — it just has to be written that way.
- **Alexandria local SEO gets delivered as scoped and described honestly.** They sell all over Egypt through their own distributors. The listing is still correct because a factory has one address, but local SEO will not move national distribution and **will not be presented as if it might.**
- **Prices and margins stay out of scope, on Aly's call.** Clean resolution rather than a gap: the goal is engagement, not revenue, so range weighting runs off the engagement goal and best-seller status instead of margin. The one consequence is that TSA answers a price comment with "price varies by shop," permanently.

## Session 5 — 9:29 PM: Facebook primary, and page 4 stops being sendable

Aly's call on the platform question: **Facebook as primary.** Recording it was a minute of work. **Checking what it contradicted was the actual job, and it turned up four defects in a deliverable that is rendered and waiting to go out.**

### The decision

| Platform | Followers | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook** | ~52,000 | **Primary. Every asset lands here first.** |
| **TikTok** | ~500 | **Secondary build**, on the character content |
| **Instagram** | ~40 | **Mirror only.** No bespoke production until it earns attention |

The reasoning that has to survive being questioned later: the client says the buyers are mainly kids, and **a 52,000-follower Egyptian Facebook page is a parent audience, not a kid one.** Facebook still wins month 1 because it is the only asset that exists, because the parent audience is exactly right for BeBo-as-school-drink and for AlRawy, and because spreading evenly from 52,000 / 500 / 40 would spend the retainer proving nothing. **TikTok is the build, not the bet.**

### Then I went and read what the kit already says, and it says the opposite

`kit.py` page 4, shipped inside `Alex Foods - Brand and Social Kit v1.2.pdf`:

> **Instagram — Reels + posts · 5 / week · Main platform.**

**On an account with forty followers.** Written before any follower number existed, which is the honest explanation and not much of a defence.

### The one that is actually serious, and it is not the platform ordering

I added up what page 4 asks for: 5 Instagram posts a week, **daily** Instagram stories, 4 Facebook posts a week, 3–4 TikToks. **That is roughly 83 posts a month.**

**Package A funds 20 assets a month** — 2 animations, 6 animated stills, 12 graphics. Eight motion pieces and twelve static.

So the kit promises a cadence the contract cannot pay for, by a factor of four, and **the page never mentions repurposing**, which is the only thing that could reconcile the two. "Reels for reach" implies motion, and the retainer makes **eight** motion pieces a month, not twenty-one.

**A rhythm the contract cannot fund is a rhythm the client can hold us to.** Clause 4 says a delivery matching the approved foundation guide counts as delivered — and this would have been the approved guide. We would have signed ourselves up to it.

**The producible version, derived from the 20 assets rather than from ambition:**

| Platform | Rhythm | From |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook | **4–5 / week** | All 20 assets. 20 ÷ 4.35 weeks. The ceiling, and exactly what the contract funds. |
| TikTok | **2 / week** | The 8 motion pieces, repurposed vertical. No extra production. |
| Instagram | **Mirrors Facebook** | Same assets. No extra production. |
| Stories | **1–2 / week** | The story-format share of the 12 graphics. |

**Daily stories cannot be saved by arithmetic.** They are not producible from this retainer at all. Either they come off, or the page says plainly that stories beyond the produced ones are zero-production — polls, questions, resharing comments — and belong to whoever runs the page day to day.

### Two more, found in the same pass

1. **The NEVER column prints "BeBo is a powder" as a fact.** As of this afternoon that is `[CONFLICT]`. **A client-facing governance document asserting a product format we cannot prove is the worst possible place for that sentence**, and it would have gone out under our name.
2. **The company-age rule is stale.** It reads "never claim an age, their own materials say 20 and 25." Settled today at 20+. The rule should now be **never claim 25**, with «أكتر من ٢٠ سنة» confirmed and usable.
