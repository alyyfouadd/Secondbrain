## Session 1 — 11:05 PM (18 Sep) to 2:25 AM: Rebuilding the kit on white, with TSA's actual information

Aly: *"Make the bg white and thats not what i need i mesnt the layout and the infoo inside it."* **Two corrections on the same deliverable, and both were mine to own.** First I read the kits as a restyle of the governance document and argued against it. Then I built a kit that had TSA's *visual* language but my own governance content poured into it — colour maths, ΔE figures, architecture theory. He wanted TSA's kit: its structure **and** the kinds of information inside it.

### What Got Done

- **Rebuilt `kit.py` on the Paper ground `#FAF8F3`**, using the client's own governed neutrals from [[Colour System]] §7 rather than anything new: Ink 17.36:1, Graphite 8.71:1, Slate at 3.41:1 marked display-only, Silver for rules, Mist for panels.
- **Mapped TSA's kit slot for slot** instead of inventing a structure:

| TSA | Alex Foods |
|---|---|
| The Guarantee (red panel) | **The line already in market** — their own «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز» |
| What makes TSA different | Four separate brands vs one company, four ranges |
| Brand pillars: Quality / Results / Guarantee | **Range / Character / Alexandria** |
| Brand tagline hero | The rule that governs every layout |
| Photo and footage templates | **Product templates** — for a food brand the pack IS the creative |

- **The pillars are observable facts, not promises, and that was deliberate.** TSA's three are claims a service business can make about itself. A food company cannot make claims without the client's written backing, so Alex Foods' three are things you can verify off the packaging: four ranges and eighteen SKUs, a character on every range, and a company whose mark carries the Pharos lighthouse and whose name is the city. **Alexandria is the one no competitor can copy, and it needs no claim to be true.**
- **Shipped `Alex Foods - Brand and Social Kit v1.1.pdf`** — 6 pages, 3.39 MB. v1.0 removed; it was superseded the same day.

### The fourth defect, and it is the same defect as the other three

`.tyl p` has specificity (0,1,1). `.d1` has (0,1,0). So **a 21pt display statement rendered at 7.2pt** and looked like nothing more than a timid design choice. I set it to 18pt, then 21pt, and the render did not change — because the rule was never winning.

Traps 4, 5 and 7 in the README are now recorded as **one trap wearing three coats: a CSS rule silently beating the one you meant.** A descendant `display:block`, a reused class name, a specificity override. **None of them error. None of them look broken in a screenshot.** The only thing that finds them is walking the DOM and reading `getComputedStyle` against what you intended. That method has now caught four real defects in this build that repeated passes of looking at PNGs missed every time.

### What I got wrong, twice, and what it cost

**Reading one:** treated "it should look like this" as a request to restyle the Design System, and spent a turn arguing against it on the basis of a locked decision. The decision was real, but it was not what he was asking about.

**Reading two:** built the kit with TSA's visual devices and my own content — contrast verdicts stamped on frames, ΔE figures, brand-architecture theory. **Governance information in a kit's clothing.** A kit is for somebody who has to make a post on Tuesday, not for somebody adjudicating whether a post is compliant.

**The lesson worth keeping: when a reference document is handed over, the information architecture is part of the reference, not just the styling.** "Make it look like this" means the sections too.

### Decisions Made

- **Paper ground resolves the §6 question entirely.** [[TSA Brand System]] §6 wants Cold White for a client deliverable, and the kit and the Design System now sit on the same ground. The departure I recorded last session is gone rather than argued.
- **`#05004B` survives as the hero-panel and sign-off colour**, so the master is still present on a light page.
- **Red stays display-only.** 4.41:1 on Paper clears 3:1 and misses 4.5:1, so the red panel carries display type and its footnote sits below it on the ground. The kit does not break its own rule on its own page.

- **Kit went from 6 pages to 8**, re-rendered and shipped as `Alex Foods - Brand and Social Kit v1.2.pdf`, 10.2 MB — inside the 15 MB WhatsApp limit in [[Brand Book Spec]] §3.

### The logo I should never have made

"Remove alex foods secondary logo" turned out to be bigger than a deletion. **There was no sanctioned alternate Alex Foods mark — I had copied TSA's pattern and fabricated one.** Out came the red-circle alternate profile picture, the "on red" lockup variant, the typed badge on the statement panel, the red-bar wordmark in the footer, and the `ALEX FOODS` corner bug on every single mockup.

**That corner bug was breaking a rule the system states in print.** The Design System page 7 says *no brand name in this system is ever set in a font*, and the bug was "ALEX FOODS" typed in Plex beside a red bar. It looked like a lockup. It was a fabrication, and I had put it on nine frames. The mockups now carry the actual seal artwork, and page 1 states plainly that there is one mark and only one.

**Worth keeping: copying a reference document's *devices* is not the same as copying its system.** TSA's kit has a sanctioned red-circle alternate because [[TSA Brand System]] §4 sanctions one. Alex Foods has no such rule, so reproducing the device invented a mark the client does not own.

### The AlRawy correction, and it was wrong in three places

**AlRawy is ready to drink.** Every pouch prints `Insert Straw Here / أدخل الماصة هنا` across the top, visible in the client's own mockup. **BeBo is the powder you make up; AlRawy is the one you open and drink.** Fixed in the kit, in [[Alex Foods Brands]] and in [[Brand Voice Guide]] §4, which now forbids any AlRawy copy implying mixing, filling or preparing — that is BeBo's story and using it describes the wrong product.

### Decisions Made

- **Packshots keyed to transparency rather than dropped in as white rectangles.** On a Paper ground `#FAF8F3` a pure-white PNG box is visible, and it would have read as a mistake on every page.
- **AlRawy's lineup keeps the range logo in it.** Its ribbon runs down into the pack row, so there is no clean cut — and it is the client's own composed artwork rather than a defect.
- **POLEKA's cola SKU appears on the range page but carries the standing warning.** It stays out of paid campaigns and out of copy until the rights are confirmed. The trademark read is confirmed against the new mockup: a contour bottle in red-and-white livery with "Cola Cola" in near-identical script.

## Session 3 — 6:00 PM: The discovery brief, and the hole underneath the Foundation

Aly handed over a client-brief template for Alex Foods, mostly `[MISSING]`, with one instruction: fill it with what we know, don't leave it missing, and add whatever else the brief needs. Read the whole vault's Alex Foods layer first — the signed scope note, the brands inventory, the roadmap, the voice guide, the kit, the slogans method, the books — then wrote it.

Shipped as **[[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]]** in `02 - TSA/`, 14 sections.

### What Got Done

- **Filled every section the vault could actually source**, and tagged each line by where it came from rather than asserting it flat.
- **Added a fourth status tag, `[OBSERVED]`**, and it is the one that makes the file honest. Roughly half of what TSA knows about this client was read off their packaging, not said by them. `[CONFIRMED]` would make a mockup read as a client statement; `[ASSUMED]` would throw away real evidence. It is its own class, and in front of the client it gets said as *"your packaging says X"*, never *"you told us X."*
- **Wrote the Arabic interview script** under every section, in Egyptian colloquial, ready to send as-is.
- **Ranked fifteen open questions** by what they change, not by section order. The first four change what TSA builds; the rest change how well it works.
- Added four items to [[Active Priorities]] and indexed the note in [[TSA]].
- **Fixed drift in [[TSA Money]]:** the revenue table still had Foundation instalment 1 due 22 Sep. It was invoiced 18 Sep, due 26 Sep, and [[Alex Foods]] says explicitly that the invoice supersedes the scope date. Two notes disagreeing about when money is owed is exactly the kind of small rot that gets believed later.

### Three corrections the template itself was carrying

1. **Five product families is four.** The template listed BeBo, AlRawy, Freeze Pops, Poleka and 2 Man. **"Freeze Pops" is 2MAN** — the contract's generic name for the range the client calls 2MAN, Arabic تومان. There is no fifth family, and counting one would have put a phantom brand into a strategy deck.
2. **"Juice factory" undersells them.** The seal reads الشركة الإسكندرية لتعبئة وتغليف المواد الغذائية — a food packing and packaging company running powdered drinks, nectar, ice pops and jelly candy. Four categories, not one.
3. **"Owner: Hussein" is not confirmed anywhere in this vault.** No signed acceptance page, no named approver, no contact record. Carried into the file as the interview subject because Aly supplied the name, but tagged `[MISSING]` with the reason stated rather than promoted to fact on a template's say-so.

### The actual finding, and it is bigger than the file

**The entire commercial layer of this client is unknown.** No stated goal. No sales figures. No prices to shop or consumer. No margins. No distribution footprint. No competitor set. No ad budget, on a contract where TSA manages the campaigns. No reporting definition, because the signed scope never wrote one.

The Foundation survives that, and it is worth being precise about why: colour, type and voice are **governance** work, correct regardless of the goal. **The content calendar and the paid campaigns are not.** Twelve graphics a month have to point somewhere.

**And the sharpest version of it: nobody has established whether this is trade marketing or consumer marketing.** A factory earns through shop owners and distributors. If the bottleneck is "we can't get into shops," then a feed full of cartoon characters does nothing for a distributor deciding whether to carry a carton, and the 40/30/20/10 content mix in [[Brand and Social Kit]] gets rewritten. That mix was derived from packaging in the honest absence of a stated goal. It was the right call with what was on the table. It is not a substitute for asking.

### Decisions Made

- **The file lives in `02 - TSA/`, not `clients/alex-foods/`.** The template asked for that path; this vault keeps one client note flat until there are three, then they all move to `Clients/` together. Forking the convention for a single file is how a map stops being true. Recorded in the note itself so a future session doesn't re-litigate it.
- **No competitor names were invented, and the table ships empty on purpose.** TSA has no sourced competitor information for this client. Naming Egyptian FMCG brands out of general knowledge would be fabricating facts in a file whose own rule forbids it, and a wrong name in front of a man who has fought these competitors for twenty years costs more than the table is worth. **What went in instead is a method:** the competitive set for ice pops and sachets sits in a freezer and on a shelf in Alexandria, and two shop visits with a phone camera produces a real audit with prices and shelf position, for nothing. That is also the competitor read deliverable 8 already asks for.
- **Seasonality is flagged as inference and labelled as such.** Four ranges probably have four different seasons — 2MAN on summer, BeBo plausibly on Ramadan, POLEKA and AlRawy plausibly on the school year. If that shape is even roughly right, one flat calendar across four ranges is wrong. **It is reasoning from product category, not from his data**, and it says so before it touches anything.
