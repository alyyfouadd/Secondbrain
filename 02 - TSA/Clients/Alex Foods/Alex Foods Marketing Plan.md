# Alex Foods — Marketing Plan

**The strategy layer for [[Alex Foods]].** Written 21 September 2026, built on [[Marketing]] — `jareds-takes.md`, `the-fundamentals.md`, `marketing-fb-ads.md` and `marketing-analytics.md` were read before a line of this was written.

> **Why this note exists.** The client's own answer to *"what is your biggest problem"* was **«weak online presence, no engagement, no marketing plan»** ([[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §1). TSA has since built a content plan, a campaign brief, a platform decision, a voice guide and a production plan — **and no document that says what the marketing is actually trying to do.** Five notes hold five halves of an argument nobody has stated in one place. This is that statement.

## 1. The strategy, in one sentence

> **Alex Foods does not need a campaign. It needs the one it already printed to become visible, and the scan at the end of it to land somewhere Alex Foods owns.**

Three facts, all confirmed, produce it:

1. **The mechanic already exists and is already shipped.** A QR code on the back of every pack for consumers, a gift coupon in every carton for retailers ([[Giveaway Programme]] §3). Printed, distributed, running.
2. **Nobody knows it exists.** It appears in none of their content. A customer holding the pack has no reason to turn it over.
3. **The scan is the only mechanism in this entire business that converts a stranger into a contact Alex Foods owns** ([[Content Plan]] §2). Everything else — the whole Facebook following included — is rented land, and the playbook is blunt about what rented land is worth.

**So the whole account points at one physical act: a hand turning a packet over.** That is the CTA, on every pillar, in every month. It is unusual, it is not "link in bio", and it is the reason this plan is not a generic FMCG content calendar.

### What that means in practice

- **The mechanic explainer is the most important asset TSA makes.** Not the brand film. [[Content Plan]] §5 already assigns A2 and G3 to it and says *"A2 before A1 if only one lands on time"* — that instruction comes from this strategy, not from a scheduling preference.
- **A post that does not eventually put a hand on a pack is a dead end**, however well it performs.
- **If the QR lands nowhere, the strategy has no back half.** §5 and §11.

## 2. Positioning

**Written as observable fact, never as claim.** A food company cannot make claims without the client's written backing ([[Brand Voice Guide]] §7), and TSA runs the ad account that would carry them.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Pocket-money drinks and frozen treats for Egyptian children, plus one lunchbox nectar for the mother who packs it |
| **Frame of reference** | The sachet rack and the shop freezer — **not the juice aisle.** The comparison a buyer actually makes is against whatever else costs the same and sits within reach of a child's hand |
| **Who it is for** | The child at the counter with their own money — BeBo, 2MAN, POLEKA. The mother filling a lunchbox — AlRawy. **Two different people and they are never addressed the same way** |
| **Point of difference** | **Range · Character · Alexandria** — the three pillars, each verifiable off the packaging, governed in [[Brand and Social Kit]] §5 |
| **Reason to believe** | 20+ years, their own distributor network, national distribution, one factory in Alexandria. **«أكتر من ٢٠ سنة» is confirmed and usable; 25 is not** |
| **The line already in market** | **«طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز»** — the client's own, and it works. **It does not get replaced to justify a deliverable.** Same rule that protects «عيش جو المغامرة» |

### What Alex Foods is deliberately NOT positioned as

**This list is the ad account's insurance policy, and each line has a live cause.**

- **Not a health brand.** No health benefit, no nutrition argument, no "good for them."
- **Not a juice brand.** AlRawy's own pouches print **Nectar**; BeBo's print **«مشروب بطعم X» / "X Drink"**. «عصير طبيعي» and «١٠٠٪» are banned in every language and on every surface.
- **Not organic.** The client called AlRawy organic; **organic is a certified claim, not a describing word**, and no certificate has been seen ([[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §A2).
- **Not a licensed-character brand.** Their pinned post is built on SpongeBob. **TSA does not produce, boost or repost third-party character content**, licence or no licence.

> **Why these sit in a positioning section rather than a legal appendix:** a positioning that quietly relies on a claim you cannot evidence is a positioning that collapses the first time a competitor screenshots it or Meta reviews the account. **The pillars above were chosen because none of them need permission to be true.**

## 3. The feeling, per range — and what it costs to get wrong

**The four feelings are owned by [[Slogans and Song]] §3** and they drive every post in [[Content Plan]]. They are not restated here.

**What belongs here is the strategic consequence:** the four ranges are four different products sold to two different people with four different feelings, **and the only thing that makes them one company is the layout, the master mark and the account they post from.** There is no house palette, by the client's own instruction — the clash is what separates them on a shelf ([[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §10).

**So the brand system is doing load-bearing work the content cannot do.** That is the actual argument for the Foundation, and it is the sentence to use at delivery.

### The cannibalisation problem nobody priced

**POLEKA and 2MAN share a freezer, a price point and a buyer.** Two Alex Foods ranges competing for one purchase. The only difference a child sees through a freezer door is the gesture — **2MAN is a stick you lick, POLEKA is a bottle you drink.**

> **The rule this sets for every asset:** POLEKA leans on the bottle and the animal and **never on cold**, which is 2MAN's territory. A POLEKA post that sells "cold" is an ad for 2MAN paid for out of POLEKA's slot.

## 4. Who we are actually talking to

**The sharpest fact in the whole engagement, and it is a mismatch:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The audience they have** | The Facebook following, counted in [[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §7. A five-year-old Egyptian Facebook page at that size is **a parent audience** |
| **The audience they describe** | *"mainly kids"* — and kids are on TikTok, where the account has ~500 followers |
| **The audience nobody has a tone for** | **The retailer.** The giveaway has a trade track and every tone in [[Brand Voice Guide]] §3–§6 speaks to a mother, a buyer or a child |

**Decision already taken: Facebook is primary** ([[Decisions]] #10), and the reasoning is in [[Brand and Social Kit]] §3b.

**What this plan adds is the implication, which has not been stated:**

> **The child is not reached through a platform. The child is reached through the pack and through the parent.** TSA has a page full of parents and a product the child asks for by name. **So month 1 sells the parent on permission and the school morning, and the pack does the selling to the child at the counter.** Chasing the kid audience on the TikTok account as it stands would spend the retainer proving nothing.
>
> **TikTok is the build, not the bet.** The characters are drawn and paid for, they are native to the platform, and they cost nothing extra because the 8 motion pieces are already funded.

## 5. The funnel Alex Foods actually has

[[Content Plan]] §2 owns the chain. **What this note adds is the map against the five stages in `the-fundamentals.md`** — because once it is drawn, the client's two "conflicting" goals stop conflicting.

| Stage | What it is here | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** | The feed. Product, character, moment, company | **Have it.** Funded at 20 assets a month |
| **Lead magnet** | **The giveaway entry — the QR scan on the back of the pack** | **Exists physically. Destination unknown.** §11 |
| **Tripwire** | **The pack itself.** A pocket-money sachet is already the cheapest possible first purchase — it *is* free-plus-shipping, in a shop | **Running, and outside TSA's control** |
| **Core offer** | Becoming a regular. The same child, the same shop, every week | Measured only through the client's own sales, which TSA does not see |
| **Profit maximiser** | **The trade.** A distributor carrying more SKUs, in more shops | **Their own stated goal**, and §8 |

### What this settles

**The client gave two goals and they looked like a contradiction: "engagement and online presence" and "mostly more shops"** ([[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §A3).

> **They are not two goals. They are the top and the bottom of one funnel.** An engaged page is the trade argument — a distributor deciding whether to carry a carton is reassured by visible demand. **Engagement is the contracted KPI. Shops are the business case. The monthly report carries both**, and §9 says exactly how.

### And the gap that decides whether any of it compounds

**The lead-magnet stage is the only one that builds an asset Alex Foods keeps.** Followers are rented. A scan that lands on a list is owned.

**Right now nobody on TSA's side knows where that QR points**, which means the most valuable thing three months could build may be getting thrown away every time somebody uses it. **This is the highest-value unanswered question on the account and it outranks any amount of content planning.**

## 6. Channels

**Weighting and rhythm are owned by [[Brand and Social Kit]] §3b.** The strategic reading:

- **Facebook is the only channel that exists.** It is the only place the contracted KPI can move inside a month, and the parent audience is exactly right for BeBo-as-school-drink and for AlRawy.
- **TikTok is a zero-marginal-cost build** off the 8 funded motion pieces and the already-drawn characters.
- **Instagram mirrors. No bespoke production until it earns attention.**
- **The shop shelf is a channel and it is not in the contract.** The pack is the highest-circulation media Alex Foods owns, it already carries the mechanic, and TSA has no control over it. Worth saying out loud at delivery. *(The Alexandria shelf walk that would have read it was dropped by [[Decisions]] #29.)*
- **Google Business Profile and local SEO are scoped to Alexandria against national distribution.** They get delivered as bought and **they do not get oversold.** Deliverable 8 is worth what it is worth; presenting it as a national growth lever is how a month-three conversation goes bad ([[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §A5).

> **The supply constraint that governs all of it:** the kit's posting rhythm asks for roughly 84 slots a month and the contract funds 20 original assets. **It only closes at ~2.7× cross-posting with zero slack**, and the client's own winner footage is the slack. Worked through in [[Content Plan]] §3. **If it will not close, the rhythm gets cut, not the quality.**

## 7. Campaign architecture

**The three campaigns, their prizes and their priority order are owned by [[Giveaway Programme]] §2.** The 90-day arc is owned by [[Content Plan]] §4.

**The architectural decision this plan makes:**

> **One giveaway platform with a swappable prize, not three campaigns.** One name, one lockup, one song structure, one set of rules, with the prize as the only variable. School gifts drop in now; the bike and scooter drop in when it resumes; Umrah drops in when it comes off the shelf.

**Three reasons, and the first is commercial:** it is what the contract priced — one slogan, one lockup. It makes the programme recognisable when a customer meets it three times in a year. And campaign two then costs a swap rather than a build.

**The exception, decided now while it is cheap:** **Umrah does not fit a platform built for children's prizes.** A pencil case and a pilgrimage cannot wear the same treatment. Umrah gets its own register — quiet, plain, no characters, no exclamation marks, closer to AlRawy's restraint than POLEKA's noise. **Decided in month 2, not in month 3 when it launches.**

**The timing collision is live and it is not a planning problem, it is a decision:** the client's number one campaign is racing the start of term, which lands **before** Foundation delivery, and the lockup is a Package A item on a month that has not started. Options and the recommendation are in [[Giveaway Programme]] §7.2; the open decision sits in [[Active Priorities]].

## 8. The trade track — the most under-used asset on the account

**Half of what the client said they want is "mostly more shops," and the signed scope contains no trade deliverable at all.** But they already own the instrument:

> **The Umrah programme is a distributor loyalty scheme.** «أعلى العملاء في حجم المسحوبات» — highest customers by volume drawn — **is a wholesaler, not a child.** They are already running a trade incentive and a consumer giveaway on one page, which also answers the trade-versus-consumer question: they do both, already.

**What this plan does with it, inside scope:**

- **Repoint the 10% "Company" slice of the content mix at the trade.** The factory, the range, the Alexandria heritage, the carton — aimed at a shop owner rather than a mother. **The rest of the mix is unchanged.** Cheapest possible way to serve both stated goals without touching the contract.
- **"More shops" needs a starting number or it can never be shown to have happened.** A rough distributor and stockist count is one answer from Mohamed and it is on the ask list.
- **Log every stockist or distributor enquiry the page produces, from month 1.** It costs nothing and it is the line that connects the engagement KPI to the business case in month three.

**What is NOT inside scope, and must be quoted under clause 7 before a minute goes into it:** trade posters, a wholesaler broadcast, carton inserts, a distributor one-pager, a price-list layout. **[[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §13 already names trade-facing material as the most likely next sale.** It is also the work most likely to be assumed rather than agreed, because the client will read "more shops" as something they already bought.

> **And a voice gap goes with it:** if TSA produces anything retailer-facing, [[Brand Voice Guide]] needs a trade register it does not have. **That is a scope question before it is a writing question.**

## 9. Paid media

**Nothing in the vault owns this and Package A sells it every month.** Structure below; the numbers are deliberately absent and §9.4 says why.

### 9.1 What the ads are actually for

**Ads are top of funnel. They buy attention, not sales.** There is nothing to sell online here — the transaction happens in a shop, for pocket money, and no ad closes it. So **100% of spend is awareness and audience-building**, and that is not a compromise, it is the correct use of the channel for this business.

**Every pound of spend has exactly two jobs:**

1. **Teach the mechanic** — turn the packet over, scan the code.
2. **Fill the pixel**, so months 2 and 3 have lookalikes worth targeting.

### 9.2 The temperature ladder

| Audience | Message | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Character content and the school moment. **No offer, no ask.** Video, because a video ad is watched in the feed and builds a viewership audience off it | Video views · engagement. **Pixel-building is the point** |
| **Warm** — page engagers, 25%+ video viewers | **The mechanic explainer.** A2 and G3 | Reach · saves and shares |
| **Hot** — engaged with a giveaway post, or scanned | **Deadline and winners.** The closing reminder, once, plainly | Engagement · the scan |

**Cold does not mean sell.** The classic way to light money on fire here would be a hard giveaway push at strangers who have never heard of the brand.

### 9.3 Targeting, testing, scaling

- **Start broad, because there is nothing to build a lookalike from yet.** No pixel history, no list. Broad until people are pixeled, then lookalikes off page engagers and off **percentage-watched** video audiences — **never off thru-plays**, which Meta counts at about three seconds and which are not viewers.
- **Geo: Alexandria and the Delta first**, where distribution is densest and the GBP work is pointed, then widen only where the client confirms stockists. **Advertising a product into a governorate that cannot buy it is the most expensive mistake available on this account.**
- **Three or four creative variations, never more.** Past four, Meta stops distributing budget evenly and no winner is readable.
- **Copy first, then image.** Find the winning line, then test images against it — the line is already written, in [[Slogans and Song]] and the captions in [[Content Plan]] §5.
- **Ramp a winner slowly:** no more than ~25% every two or three days. Cranking a winner's budget kills its performance on the spot.
- **Turn off anything performing worse than a sibling.** There is no reason to pay more for a worse ad.

### 9.4 The measurement hole — settle this before the first pound

> **Entry is a QR scan on a pack bought in a shop. Meta cannot see it.** So unless the QR destination is instrumented, campaigns can only be optimised toward reach, engagement or clicks — **none of which is the actual outcome — and TSA cannot evidence a single entry it drove.**

**The fix, and it is small if it is done before launch:** the QR resolves to a page TSA can instrument — Meta pixel on it, a UTM per campaign — so a scan becomes a countable event and a retargetable audience. **That turns the invisible outcome into the primary one and it turns the giveaway into the list-building machine §5 says it should be.**

**Two honest caveats.** If nothing is built behind the QR, building it is **clause 7 work and gets quoted** — it is not in either stage. And if the client will not or cannot instrument it, **the campaign objective changes and the reporting promise changes with it**, in writing, before spend, not in month two when someone asks what the money bought.

### 9.5 Why there are no numbers in this section

**No budget has been named.** TSA is contracted to manage a spend nobody has stated, and a structure for a small monthly budget and one for a large one are not the same structure.

**And no baseline exists.** The contracted KPI is comments and views; TSA holds follower counts and nothing else, and a follower count says nothing about either. **Quoting a benchmark cost-per-result from general knowledge would be invented precision** — exactly what this vault exists to prevent. When Meta admin lands, the benchmark comes from their own reporting.

> **So the thresholds get set at the end of week 2 of month 1, off real data, and the framework is written now so nobody has to have an opinion later.** §10.

## 10. Measurement, and the monthly report

**The signed scope defines no reporting cadence, no metrics and no format.** Agreeing it now costs nothing; arguing in month three about whether TSA proved anything costs the renewal. **This is TSA's proposal to put in the delivery message.**

### The contracted number

**Engagement rate — interactions ÷ reach, per post, on a rolling 28 days.** Not followers. Followers are the vanity metric the client did not ask for, and **no follower number gets promised** on a page that has sat idle.

**Baseline = weeks 2–4 of month 1.** Week 1 is launch noise and always flatters. **The month-3 goal and the three verdicts that hang off it are owned by [[Content Plan]] §7** and are not restated here.

### The report itself — one document, same date every month

| Section | What is in it |
|---|---|
| **The number** | Engagement rate against baseline, rolling 28 days, with the verdict from the thresholds table |
| **Reach and views** | Their own words for success, reported in their own words |
| **Audience** | Follower movement, stated as context and never as the goal |
| **Paid** | Spend, result, cost per result against our own baseline once one exists |
| **Scans** | **If and only if the QR is instrumented.** Otherwise the line reads *"not measurable — see §9.4"* every month until it is fixed |
| **Trade** | **Every stockist or distributor enquiry the page produced.** The line that connects the KPI to the business case |
| **The change** | What we are doing differently next month, and why |

### Three rules that go with it

1. **Read every number through context before reacting.** Seasonal? Something outside our control? Did we change something on purpose? A rolling window, never a single day.
2. **A shocking number is a reason to check the plumbing first.** If a metric reads zero or goes flat, check the tracking before checking the strategy.
3. **Decide the thresholds in advance and let the number make the call.** The staring stops when the metric makes the decision.

> **And the number that decides the renewal is not on that table.** It is the QR list. *"Engagement rate rose 1.5×"* is a much weaker sentence in a renewal conversation than *"you now have a database of your own customers."*

## 12. What is NOT in this plan, and the scope line

**A marketing plan is not one of the eight Brand Foundation deliverables and it is not in Package A.** The signed scope buys a voice guide, a colour and type system, social pages setup, slogans and the song, a monthly content calendar, a shooting recipe, a Google Business Profile and local SEO — and then twenty assets a month plus campaign management.

> **So this note is TSA's internal strategy, and it is the right thing to build: it is what the eight deliverables get built from, and it costs nothing to have.** What it is **not** is a client-facing document TSA has agreed to produce.
>
> **The trap to avoid, and the client has already walked us toward it once:** they said their problem is *"no marketing plan."* If a formatted marketing plan goes to them as part of the Foundation, **it becomes a ninth deliverable that was never priced**, and it sets the precedent that scope expands when they name a problem. **If they want one, it is clause 7 work and it is quoted** — and it is a good sale, because it is mostly written.

**Also deliberately out:** trade-facing material (§8), a produced version of the giveaway song, a TikTok build-out beyond cross-posting, a vector redraw of the four range marks, a packaging redesign, and a product shoot. **All of them are live upsells and all of them are in [[Alex Foods Discovery Brief]] §13.**
