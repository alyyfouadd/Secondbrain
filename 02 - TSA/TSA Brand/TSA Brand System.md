# TSA Brand System

[[TSA]]'s own locked brand identity, v3.1. **Three colours, two typefaces, one tagline, one logo.** Transcribed from `TSA Brand Lock Reference v3.1.pdf` and `TSA Usage Rules.pdf` in this folder, which are the source documents.

> **This governs everything TSA makes**, including every client deliverable TSA hands over. It is TSA's own system, not a client's, and it does not change per job.
> **The default when a decision is not covered: take the more restrained option.**

---

## 1. Colour

| Name | HEX | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Precision Navy** | `#0A0F1E` | Primary background |
| **Signal Red** | `#E8203A` | Accent, reserved |
| **Cold White** | `#F0F2F5` | Type and light backgrounds |
| **Surface Navy** | `#1A2340` | Utility tone — **not a brand colour** |

**Working ratio: Precision Navy 70% · Surface 22% · Red 8%.** Cold White sits on top of all three as type. **If a layout is more than roughly a tenth red, it is wrong.**

**Precision Navy** — the default background of everything TSA makes. Also the type colour on Cold White grounds. Never as type on Surface Navy or Signal Red, never as a headline colour on any dark ground, never lightened or warmed for a particular deliverable.

**Signal Red** — the logo bar, exactly one key word per headline, CTA buttons, section eyebrows and rule lines, the guarantee panel, the invoice total row.
> **Red should read as a decision, not decoration. If you cannot say in one sentence why that specific element is red, it should not be.**

Never body copy, invoice line items, fine print, two accents in one headline, a full-bleed background other than the guarantee panel, behind Cold White text at small sizes, or as a tint, gradient or opacity variant.

**Cold White** — all headlines and body on dark grounds. **The background of any document printed or sent to a client to sign**: invoices, quotes, contracts, one-pagers. **Never pure `#FFFFFF`. Cold White is the only white in the system and it replaces white everywhere** — text, fills, borders, icons, exported assets.

**Surface Navy** — cards, panels, table rows and sidebars that need to separate from a Precision Navy page. Structure only. **Never a logo background, never type, never presented to a client as a brand colour, never listed in a palette.**

## 2. Typography — two faces, no exceptions

**Barlow Condensed 800 — display only.** Headlines and subheads, section titles, the logo wordmark, document titles, numbers and stats, CTA labels, video lower-thirds and end cards, the tagline.
**Never** paragraphs of any length, never below 8pt, **never any weight other than 800** — no Bold, no SemiBold, no Regular, no italic — and never letterspaced so wide it stops reading as a word.

**Inter — everything else.** Body copy, captions, invoice line items, fine print, table cells, email body, form labels, footers.
**Never** headlines, never the tagline, never display-size all-caps. Weight 400 for body, 500–600 for small tracked labels, 700 only inside a sentence for emphasis. **No italics for emphasis — use weight.**

### Scale

| Role | Face | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | Barlow Condensed 800 | 26–40 pt |
| Section headline | Barlow Condensed 800 | 12–15 pt |
| Stat / number | Barlow Condensed 800 | 9–22 pt |
| Eyebrow / label | Inter 600, 0.2em tracking | 4.5–5.5 pt |
| Body copy | Inter 400 | 6–7 pt |
| Fine print | Inter 400 | 5–5.5 pt |

## 3. Tagline

> **Grow the standard.**

**Set as:** Barlow Condensed 800, sentence case, **period always included**. "standard" in Signal Red on dark grounds; all Precision Navy on Cold White grounds.

**Placement:** under the logo in the sign-off lockup, separated by a gap equal to twice the height of the red bar. Email signature, invoice footer, proposal and quote covers, the closing slide of every deck, website footer, social bio, video end card. **Quiet, once per surface, at the end.**

**Never:** all-caps, set in Inter, used as a post headline in place of a hook, split across two lines, without the period, reworded ("Grow the standard with TSA"), two taglines on one surface, or repeated on the same page.

> **RETIRED: "The Only Agency In Alex That Guarantees Your Results."** Withdrawn. Remove it from every template, export, profile, signature and deck. **It is not an alternate tagline.**

## 4. Logo

| Lockup | What it is | Use | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Red bar + "THE STANDARD AGENCY" | Where the reader may not know the agency: proposal covers, invoices, contracts, website header, email signature, first slide | 32mm print / 150px screen |
| **Compact** | Red bar + "TSA" | Where the name is established or space is tight: social, story covers, video corner bugs, document footers, merchandise | 12mm print / 56px screen |
| **Sign-off** | "TSA." with the period in Signal Red | Dark grounds only. Closes a video, deck or campaign frame. **Never a header mark.** | — |
| **Profile mark** | Compact lockup centred in a circle | Precision Navy circle by default; Signal Red circle for campaigns and launches only | Export 320 × 320 |

**Clear space:** a margin equal to the height of the red bar on all four sides. **Nothing enters it** — no text, no image edge, no page edge, no second logo.

**Backgrounds:** Precision Navy, Cold White, Signal Red (bar flips to Cold White), Surface Navy, or a dark footage still under the gradient scrim.

**Never** stretched, squashed, rotated, outlined, shadowed or given a gradient. Never recoloured outside the system. **Never with the red bar removed, resized independently, or moved to the right.** Never on mid-tone or busy footage without the scrim. **Never reconstructed by typing "TSA" in another font.** Never inside a shape other than the sanctioned circle.

## 5. Drift check — send it back if any of these are true

- A hex outside `#0A0F1E` / `#E8203A` / `#F0F2F5` (Surface Navy `#1A2340` for panels only)
- Pure `#FFFFFF` used instead of Cold White
- A third typeface, or Barlow at a weight other than 800
- Red on a paragraph, or more than one red word in a headline
- The old guarantee tagline anywhere on the file
- Logo without its red bar, or with anything inside its clear space

## 6. How this applies to a client deliverable

A brand kit TSA produces for a client is **a TSA document about the client's brand.** Two systems meet in one file, and the boundary has to be explicit or the drift check fires on work that is actually correct.

**The rule: TSA's system owns the document. The client's system is the subject.**

| Layer | Whose system | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cover, section openers, footers, page furniture, eyebrows, labels, body copy | **TSA** — Precision Navy, Cold White, Signal Red, Barlow + Inter | It is TSA's document and it should read as one |
| Swatches, specimen blocks, ramps, the client's own artwork | **The client's** | They are the content. A brand book that cannot show a client's magenta is not a brand book |

**Two carve-outs from the drift check, and they are not drift:**

1. **Client hexes outside TSA's three are content, not document colour.** A swatch is an object being displayed, never page furniture. The moment a client colour becomes a header, a rule line or a background, it is drift.
2. **A client's typeface shown as a specimen is not "a third typeface."** It is a sample, clearly labelled as the client's. The moment it sets a heading or a paragraph in the document, it is drift.

**Cold White is the document ground**, because TSA's own rules already name it for "any document printed or sent to a client to sign." A brand kit is exactly that. It is the on-system choice, not a compromise.

## 7. The gap: there is no Arabic face in this system

**Barlow Condensed and Inter are Latin-only, and TSA is an Alexandria agency producing Arabic work.** Every bilingual deliverable TSA makes is therefore off-system by necessity, including the Alex Foods Design System, where Arabic headings are currently set in IBM Plex Sans Arabic as an interim.

This is not a drift-check failure by anyone. It is a hole in the locked system, and it will keep forcing ad-hoc decisions until it is named and closed.

**What closing it needs:** one Arabic display face to sit beside Barlow Condensed 800, and one Arabic text face to sit beside Inter. Both free and properly licensed, as with everything else here. Candidates worth looking at are **Alexandria** (a Google Fonts Arabic family with a matching Latin and a range of weights) and **IBM Plex Sans Arabic**, which is already in use on the Alex Foods work and pairs cleanly with Inter's neo-grotesque shapes.

**Decision needed from Aly.** Until it lands, any Arabic in a TSA document is provisional and should be flagged as such rather than quietly standardised by repetition.
