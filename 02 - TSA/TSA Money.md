# TSA Money

The agency's books. What TSA earns, what it costs to run, and what leaves it as a draw to me. All figures in EGP.

It becomes my money at one specific moment — when I draw it, deliberately, as a decided number — and that moment gets written down here. ## What the 42,000 is actually holding up

This is the number that makes the two-pot split necessary rather than tidy.

The 42,000 is **one paid month of Package A that has not been delivered.** Under clause 9 a paid month runs to its end even if either side walks, so TSA owes that month's work regardless of what happens next. Against that cash sit roughly twenty deliverables:

- 2 product animations
- 6 animated stills
- 12 graphics, feed and story
- 1 content calendar
- GBP and local SEO maintenance
- Paid campaign management
- 1 giveaway slogan and campaign lockup

> **Watch this line, 19 September.** The client is running **three** giveaway campaigns, and the contract buys **one** slogan and **one** lockup per month. Held as one platform with a swappable prize slot, it stays inside the priced month. Allowed to become three builds, or to pull in coupon artwork, a QR landing page or retailer-facing material, it is uncosted work on a month that is already paid for. **This is the likeliest place the scope quietly grows on this contract.** See [[Giveaway Programme]] §5.

In plain terms: **it is not profit, it is an advance.** Some of it has to survive long enough to pay for producing that month. How much is the one number nobody has worked out yet, and it is the gate on everything below.

**Ad spend is not a TSA cost.** The scope is explicit — media spend does not pass through TSA, the client pays the platforms directly. Never let it land in this pot or in these numbers.

### The rule I'm proposing, to be set rather than drifted into

1. **Hold back the delivery cost of any paid-but-undelivered month.** That money is already spoken for.
2. **What remains after the holdback is drawable.**
3. **A draw is a decided number on a decided date, logged in the table above** — not "whatever is in the account when a bill lands."

### What that means with today's numbers

Call the cost of delivering Package A month 1 **X**.

- Drawable now = **42,000 − X**
- Personal debts = **23,000** (see [[Money]])
- So clearing every debt from this payment alone needs **X ≤ 19,000** *(calculated)*.

If delivering that month costs more than 19,000, then paying off everything today is spending money that Alex Foods' work needs, and the shortfall shows up mid-month when there's production to fund and nothing to fund it with. If X is comfortably under 19,000, the debts clear and the month is still funded.

**Work out X. That is step one, and it is the only thing standing between "I have 42,000" and "I know what I can spend."**
