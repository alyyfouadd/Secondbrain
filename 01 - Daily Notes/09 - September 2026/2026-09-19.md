### The fourth defect, and it is the same defect as the other three

`.tyl p` has specificity (0,1,1). `.d1` has (0,1,0). So **a 21pt display statement rendered at 7.2pt** and looked like nothing more than a timid design choice. I set it to 18pt, then 21pt, and the render did not change — because the rule was never winning.

Traps 4, 5 and 7 in the README are now recorded as **one trap wearing three coats: a CSS rule silently beating the one you meant.** A descendant `display:block`, a reused class name, a specificity override. **None of them error. None of them look broken in a screenshot.** The only thing that finds them is walking the DOM and reading `getComputedStyle` against what you intended. That method has now caught four real defects in this build that repeated passes of looking at PNGs missed every time.

### What I got wrong, twice, and what it cost

**Reading one:** treated "it should look like this" as a request to restyle the Design System, and spent a turn arguing against it on the basis of a locked decision. The decision was real, but it was not what he was asking about.

**Reading two:** built the kit with TSA's visual devices and my own content — contrast verdicts stamped on frames, ΔE figures, brand-architecture theory. **Governance information in a kit's clothing.** A kit is for somebody who has to make a post on Tuesday, not for somebody adjudicating whether a post is compliant.

**The lesson worth keeping: when a reference document is handed over, the information architecture is part of the reference, not just the styling.** "Make it look like this" means the sections too.

### The BeBo conflict, and I went to the artwork rather than picking a side

**The client says BeBo is "juice in a sachet you drink directly from with a straw."** The vault has said since 18 September that BeBo is **powder, one sachet makes a jug** — and the entire voice section rests on it: the feeling is *permission*, the words-in list is ظرف and إبريق, the lead caption is «ظرف واحد يعمل إبريق كامل».

So I opened their own packs and looked properly, at the source mockup rather than the thumbnails.

**What is actually printed:** a **pillow bag**, not a hand-sized sachet. «مشروب بطعم الخوخ / Peach Drink». The peach pack carries **a cup with a straw standing in it**; the cola pack carries **a tall glass of cola over ice**. Both are pictures of a drink already poured. **And no BeBo pack carries «أدخل الماصة هنا» — AlRawy prints it across the top of every single pouch.** That print is the difference between the two ranges and it is on one of them only.

**Most likely the person answering described AlRawy and applied it to both.** The two answers are near-identical sentences.

**But I am not letting TSA off either.** The word *powder* appears nowhere on the front of the pack, and neither does a jug. **"One sachet makes a jug" is our inference**, and a pillow bag could perfectly well hold a strip of individual sachets, which would make both accounts true at once. So the honest state is that **both readings are unsupported**, and one photo of the back of a pack ends it.

**BeBo copy is frozen until that photo arrives** — and BeBo is now, by their own answer, the **priority range and the best seller**. Worst possible range to be wrong about.

### The one that is a legal problem, not a factual one

**They called AlRawy "organic juice."** Two separate issues and both land on TSA: the pack says **nectar**, and **organic is a certified claim rather than a describing word.** It is a bigger exposure than «طبيعي» because it asserts a certification nobody has seen, and **TSA runs the paid campaigns** — so an ad carrying it is TSA's ad account under review.

Banned in every language and on every surface until a certificate exists. **And the right move is to ask for it properly rather than only refuse the word** — if they hold it, it is a genuine asset and it changes AlRawy's positioning completely.

2. **The kit's Facebook weighting stops being reasoning and becomes measurement.** 52,000 against 540 across both other platforms combined.

## Session 9 — 10:55 PM: The recipe, finished

Aly: finish it, every English word in English with Arabic alongside, clean, organised, real value in it. **Written and complete as [[Shooting and Compositing Recipe]]** — Foundation deliverable 6, book section 16, twelve sections, bilingual throughout with Arabic leading.

### The rule the whole document exists to enforce

> **المشهد يُولَّد. المنتج لا يُولَّد أبدًا.**
> **The scene is generated. The product never is.**

AI does not render correct Arabic lettering and it does not redraw a registered mark accurately. **It renders a resemblance, and on a food pack a resemblance is a fabricated product that does not exist.** So the generator makes the place and the real pack is composited into it.

**That rule protects both sides at once, which is why it holds:** the pack artwork is the client's, so every pixel of product in every image is theirs. No visual claim nobody approved, no trademark redrawn wrongly.

### The technique the document turns on

> **اطلب الغرفة، لا تطلب المنتج. · Ask for the room, never for the product.**

Never prompt for "a juice pack on a table", because the generator draws a fabricated pack. **Prompt for an empty set with a clear open space, then composite the real pack into it.** Four ready prompts went in, one per range, plus the exclusion list that rides on every one of them: no text, no Arabic script, no logos, no packaging, no hands, no faces.

**Banning lettering is the most important entry.** One character in the background and the generator has invented a fake language on a food brand's image.

### What makes eighteen SKUs look like one family

**Eight constants that never change on any range:** camera 10 to 15 degrees above, 50 mm equivalent, key light upper left at 45 degrees, soft and broad, 5200 K, soft contact shadow, pack fully sharp, square capture. **The range changes the world around the pack. It never changes these.**

Then a scene world per range: BeBo a home kitchen in morning light, AlRawy a clean table in clear daylight, 2MAN wet concrete and ice under hard sun, POLEKA a flat colour ground with no realism at all.

### The two bans that are doing real work

1. **BeBo may not be shown being prepared or drunk from.** Its format is contested and **an image showing how it is used asserts an answer we do not have.** It waits on the back of pack photo, same as the copy does.
2. **The POLEKA cola SKU appears in nothing paid.** The rights are unconfirmed and TSA runs the ad account.

### The section most people would have left out

**§09, claims inside the picture.** The never say list applies to images with exactly the same force as to sentences: no gym or lab or stethoscope, no green leaves as a nature cue around a powdered product, no fruit pile implying juice content on a nectar, no school uniform implying an endorsement, no named shop front promising availability. **An image is a published claim, and a food page can be reported over a single one.**

### Craft detail worth keeping

**Seven compositing rules, and item 5 is the one that convinces the eye:** sample the scene's colour and lay it on the pack edges at 5 to 10 percent. **A pack the room's colour has not touched always looks like it came from another photograph, even when the shadow is perfect.**

### Decisions Made

- **Prompts are written in English and that is the one deliberate exception to the Arabic first rule.** Generators understand English far better, and a prompt is an internal tool nobody outside the agency sees. The document says so rather than looking inconsistent.
- **No em dashes anywhere in the deliverable.** [[Brand Voice Guide]] §1 rule 8 bans them in outgoing copy, and this is client facing. Checked: zero in the file.
- **No tool pricing written down.** Generation pricing changes constantly and an unverifiable number in a governance document is worse than none. The rule went in instead: price any subscription before choosing it, try the free option first.
- **Built for the real machine.** Generation happens inside the agency session, compositing on the iPad in a layers app. Nothing in the document needs a desktop.
