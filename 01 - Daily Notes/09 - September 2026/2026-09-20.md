## Session 1 — 3:20 AM: Their lines were the answer

Aly, on the ten rejected slogans: **"their slogans were good."**

### That is a better diagnosis than mine was

Yesterday I blamed rhythm, rhyme and idiom. **The real problem was doctrine.**

Their own in-market lines are «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز», «عيش جو المغامرة» and «جودة نثق بها منذ ٢٠ عامًا». **Every one of them names the brand or sits right next to the product.** Warm, direct, everyday comparatives, no cleverness.

**And §1 of [[Slogans and Song]] tells you never to do that.** *Never describe the product, never name it, sell the feeling.* **That rule is correct for a headline in a funnel and it is wrong for this brand in this market**, and I applied it to ten lines without ever checking it against the client's own working creative, which was sitting in `client-legacy-creative/` the whole time.

The note even warned that headline rules do not transfer wholesale to slogans. **It named specificity and urgency as the things that break. It missed the one that actually broke.**

### The consequence nobody had drawn

**If their lines are good, the master line already exists.** «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز» is in market and working.

**The same rule that protects عيش جو المغامرة protects it: replacing a working line to justify a deliverable destroys equity TSA was hired to build.** So two of the five brands are already solved, and the honest deliverable is **three lines and a giveaway, not five and a giveaway.** Smaller bill of work, better answer, and it only appeared once the register question was settled.

### The rewrite

Built on their patterns rather than against them. **مع + أحلى** is their structure, so BeBo gets «مع بيبو، البيت أحلى» and POLEKA gets «مع بوليكا، اللعب أحلى». AlRawy borrows their own trust word: **«الراوي.. طعم تثق فيه»** echoes «جودة نثق بها», which is the client's own language for exactly what AlRawy is for.

**All marked drafts for Aly's ear.** Ten lines passed every structural test yesterday and failed the only test that counts.

## Session 3 — 3:55 AM: BeBo was never talking to the mother

Aly, on «مع بيبو، البيت أحلى»: **"Not البيت, it's a kids product."**

### The correction is one word wide and a section deep

**البيت is a mother's frame.** So is the whole BeBo voice section: *talking to the mother*, the feeling *permission without the wince*, a register described as **أم لأم**, and a words-in list of **ظرف · العيال · يكفّي · البيت**. Every one of those is a parent's vocabulary about children rather than a child's vocabulary about a drink.

### And I had been told the right answer three sessions ago

The client's discovery answer, in their own words: **"the kids buy them all mainly."**

**I read that, noticed it contradicted the voice guide, wrote the contradiction down, and then deferred to my own read of the packaging.** The line is still in the brief's history: *"it sits against BeBo talking to the mother... worth one more question rather than a rewrite."*

**That was the wrong call and it is the second time this week in the same shape.** The BeBo format question was the first: their statement against our inference, and I gave our inference the benefit of the doubt both times.

> **The rule, written into the brief so the next session inherits it: when the client states who buys it, that outranks what the packaging suggests.** A pack's characters tell you who the artwork is aimed at, not who is standing at the counter.

### What changed

BeBo now talks to **the kid**. The feeling is **«طعمي أنا»** — *it is mine and it tastes good* — rather than permission, **because a kid does not need permission, they need the thing to be worth asking for.** Register is kid to kid, 4 to 9 words, emoji free to three, person direct and never through a parent. **البيت and العيال moved from the words-in list to the words-out list.**

**Three of the four ranges now talk to the child, and AlRawy is the only adult-facing one** — which is exactly what §4 has said about AlRawy since it was written. The guide is more consistent after the correction than before it.

### The new BeBo line

**«مع بيبو، الفسحة أحلى»** — their **مع + أحلى** structure kept, but **الفسحة is the child's own word for the school break**, and school is the one occasion the client named themselves when they said it sells as an affordable juice for schools. **It names no format**, so it still survives whichever way the back-of-pack photo goes.

## Session 4 — 4:10 AM: BeBo has a line, and Aly out-picked me

**«بيبو معاك كل يوم».** He took the third alternate, not the one I led with.

### He was right, and the reason is structural rather than taste

I recommended **«مع بيبو، الفسحة أحلى»** because الفسحة is the child's own word and it ties to the one occasion the client named. **That is exactly what is wrong with it.**

**It locks the brand to school.** BeBo sells all year, and school stops for three months. **An occasion-bound line goes quiet with the occasion**, which makes it a campaign line wearing a brand line's clothes. §4's own test 3 says a brand line has to hold across every context, and I recommended one that does not.

**«كل يوم» does the opposite: it claims the habit.** For an everyday affordable drink that is the whole ask, and it is the sort of claim a brand line can hold for years without ever needing a document behind it.

**The rest of it checks out too:** «معاك» is second person singular and direct, so there is no parent in between, which was the correction being made an hour earlier. It names no format, so it survives the back-of-pack answer either way. It works across all five SKUs. Brand name inside the line, in their register, no cleverness. And three beats — بي-بو · مَ-عاك · كل-يوم — so a child can repeat it.

### One territory line that fell out of it

**BeBo owns «معاك». POLEKA owns «صاحبك».** Accompaniment against companionship, and they sit close enough that if both ranges use both words the two brands blur. **POLEKA's entire mechanic is the character as a friend**, so «صاحبك» came out of BeBo's words-in list in the same pass.

### Where the deliverable stands

**Three of five settled.** The master keeps «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز» and 2MAN keeps «عيش جو المغامرة» — both theirs, both in market, both working, and neither touched. BeBo now has a line. **Outstanding: AlRawy, POLEKA, and the giveaway song.**

**Recorded as selected rather than final.** The vault's rule is that restating a draft is iterating and not signing off, so it is marked locked when he says lock.

## Session 5 — 4:25 AM: The giveaway belongs to BeBo

Aly: **BeBo is the one who does giveaways.**

**I had specified the lockup as the master's** — *"it sits on the master, not on a range, since the master is the account that posts"* — **and written the song with «أليكس فودز» in the hook.** Both wrong, and the second one was wrong in a way that mattered, because the hook is the one place the brand name has to be.

### Why BeBo is the better owner, independently of who decided it

1. **Priority range and best seller**, by the client's own answer. **A giveaway spends attention, and it should be spent where the sales already are.**
2. **The audience is kids**, who enter giveaways with a willingness no adult audience matches.
3. **Five flavours**, so entry repeats naturally and the prize rotates without inventing anything.
4. **It is the cheapest pack.** Entry costs a child almost nothing, and **a giveaway requiring an expensive purchase is a giveaway with no entrants.**

**And it fits the line chosen an hour earlier.** «بيبو معاك كل يوم» claims a daily habit, and **a weekly giveaway gives that habit a reason to be photographed.** That is the cheapest bridge there is between a slogan and a mechanic.

### The distinction that keeps the architecture intact

**The giveaway belongs to BeBo. It runs on the Alex Foods account.** Those are different questions and conflating them is what produced my error.

The master account is the only presence with an audience — 52,000 against 540 across both other platforms — so **a range campaign runs inside the master presence. That is the umbrella model working as designed, not an exception to it.** The lockup carries **BeBo's mark as campaign owner and the Alex seal, because the master owns the seal and always has.**

### The song, rewritten on BeBo

The structural move is worth keeping: **it opens and closes on the brand line.**

> «بيبو معاك كل يوم
> صوّر كيسك وابعتلنا
> كل أسبوع في فايز جديد
> بيبو معاك كل يوم»

**A giveaway song's real job is not the giveaway.** It is drilling the slogan into a child's head while the prize is motivating them to repeat it. **The prize buys the repetition; the repetition plants the line.** Mechanic in line 2, weekly cadence and urgency in line 3.

**Marked draft, and the metre specifically needs Aly's ear.** Four Arabic drafts have been wrong this week and this one is not assumed right.

### One thing flagged rather than left to happen

**A recurring weekly BeBo giveaway gives BeBo more of the month than a flat four-range rotation would.** Defensible, since it is the priority range and the best seller — **but it should be a decision in the content calendar rather than a side effect of the giveaway.** §17 carries it when seasonality lands.

## Session 6 — 4:40 AM: «صوّر واكسب مع بيبو»

Aly's line for the giveaway, and it replaces the second line of the song I drafted fifteen minutes earlier.

### Why his beats mine, nameably

**Mine was «صوّر كيسك وابعتلنا».** It named the mechanic and stopped there.

**His carries the mechanic AND the reward in three words.** **صوّر** and **اكسب** — two imperative verbs, action then payoff, nothing between them — and **«مع بيبو»** puts the brand inside the line using the client's own pattern. **That is the entire campaign proposition in a single breath**, where mine was an instruction with no reason to follow it.

### The architectural thing it gets right, which I had not articulated

**It is a campaign line, not a brand line, and the two now sit beside each other properly:**

- **«بيبو معاك كل يوم»** is the permanent claim. It runs forever.
- **«صوّر واكسب مع بيبو»** is the temporary ask. It runs while the campaign runs.

**A campaign line that carries the brand name means the campaign spends its budget on the brand rather than on itself.** That is the whole reason a campaign lockup exists as a paid deliverable, and it is why the pairing makes both lines stronger than either alone.

### The song, rebuilt

> «صوّر واكسب مع بيبو
> كل أسبوع في فايز جديد
> ابعت صورتك من دلوقتي
> بيبو معاك كل يوم»

**Opens on the campaign line, closes on the brand line.** The prize buys the child's attention at line 1 and the repetition plants «بيبو معاك كل يوم» at line 4. **When the campaign ends the brand line stays in the child's head, which is the only part of a giveaway with any residual value.**

### What it closes

**This is a Package A line item, not a Foundation one.** The scope buys *1 × giveaway slogan and campaign lockup* every month. **The slogan half is now written.** The lockup is still to be designed, and it carries BeBo's mark as campaign owner plus the Alex seal, because the master owns the seal.

## Session 7 — 5:05 AM: A plan with dates, and a brief that reports against it

### The gap that had been open since 18 September

**The vault has had a sequenced build order for two days and not one date on it.** [[Foundation Roadmap]] says what order things go in and what each depends on. **It never said when.**

**That is how a three-week job becomes a four-week job without anybody deciding it should**, and it matters here because two contractual dates already exist: **instalment 2 falls 8 October and delivery was targeted at 9 October.** Delivery has to come *before* the money, not after it.

**[[Alex Foods Delivery Plan]]** now carries nineteen days, day by day, with what each one depends on.

**The shape:** week 1 builds every section that needs nothing from the client, and **sends the client ask on day one rather than "this week"** — because four of the five things that can break this are one message away. Week 2 takes the blocked sections or ships them with an honest chip, **because a blocked section does not delay the book.** Week 3 assembles, proofs twice in different orders, and delivers on the 9th.

**Friday 2 October is deliberately empty.** A plan with no slack is a plan that fails on its first bad day.

### The daily brief

**Five lines, never more.** Date and countdown · today's item from the plan · what actually moved · what is blocking and **who by name** · money inside seven days. Then one line naming the single most useful thing to do first.

**The rules are what make it worth reading:** never invent progress, **and if nothing moved, say nothing moved** — a brief that always reports progress is a brief nobody believes. Never pad; a quiet day gets three lines. No greeting and no closing offer, because it is a status line rather than a message. **Overdue gets called overdue**, not *still outstanding*.

**18:00 Cairo, scheduled as a Routine.** **Aly works nights and sleeps around 8 AM, so six in the evening is the start of his working day rather than the end of it.** A brief landing at 9 AM arrives while he is asleep and is stale before he reads it.

### Decisions Made
- **The plan is dated to the money, not to the work.** Delivery on the 9th sits before the 8 October instalment because **chasing an invoice after delivering is a worse conversation than chasing it before.**
- **The brief reads the vault rather than remembering.** Its prompt names the five files it must open every time.
- **One empty day is in the plan on purpose.**
