## Session 1 — 3:10 AM: The winner-post template

*Continues the run that started on the evening of 20 September; this session crossed midnight Cairo, so it takes its own day per the rule.*

### What Got Done

- **Aly approved four winner posts a month** and asked for a template rather than four layouts. Logged as `Decisions.md` #32.
- **Built it as §6 of `Month 1 Asset Brief.md`** — not a new note, because that note already owns month-1 production, and not a second copy inside `Post Archetypes.md`, which now links to it instead. **The archetype says what the post is; the template says how it gets built.**
- **The constraint the whole template is designed around: TSA does not control the photograph and never will.** These are phone shots in shops — bad light, cluttered counters, an arm in frame. **The template's job is to rescue a mediocre photo, not decorate a good one**, and every rule in it follows from that.
- **The rule that outranks the rest, written at the top: the photo is the hero and gets at least 60% of the frame.** The standard failure in this category is an agency burying the proof under its own branding. **If it reads as a graphic with a photo in it, it has failed.**
- **Type never sits on the photograph.** One decision, and it is what makes an unpredictable source image survivable — legibility stops being a gamble on every incoming file.
- **A source-photo accept-and-reject gate**, which is the part that will actually save time: reject if the prize is not in the winner's hands, if the short side is under ~1000 px, if a competitor's product is visible, or if **a shop sign is legible enough to read as an endorsement** — a photograph names a shop as loudly as a caption does, and the never-say list forbids it.
- **The Umrah variant specified now rather than in month 3.** No range mark, no character, no exclamation, Ink rule instead of a colour one. **A pilgrimage cannot wear the treatment that sells jelly sweets.**
- **The consent rule, and it is not a design preference.** Most winners on a kids product are children. **Consent to receive a prize is not consent to appear in an advert, and a child cannot give that consent at all** — so it is the parents' written permission or the post does not run. The queue item was rewritten to say so.
- **"Two winners in one post" added to the never list.** Four separate posts are four proofs; one grid of four is one proof. **The format's whole value is repetition.**
