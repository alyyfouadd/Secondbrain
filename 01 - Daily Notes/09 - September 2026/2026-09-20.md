## Session 1 — 3:20 AM: Their lines were the answer

Aly, on the ten rejected slogans: **"their slogans were good."**

### That is a better diagnosis than mine was

Yesterday I blamed rhythm, rhyme and idiom. **The real problem was doctrine.**

Their own in-market lines are «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز», «عيش جو المغامرة» and «جودة نثق بها منذ ٢٠ عامًا». **Every one of them names the brand or sits right next to the product.** Warm, direct, everyday comparatives, no cleverness.

**And §1 of `Slogans and Song` tells you never to do that.** *Never describe the product, never name it, sell the feeling.* **That rule is correct for a headline in a funnel and it is wrong for this brand in this market**, and I applied it to ten lines without ever checking it against the client's own working creative, which was sitting in `client-legacy-creative/` the whole time.

The note even warned that headline rules do not transfer wholesale to slogans. **It named specificity and urgency as the things that break. It missed the one that actually broke.**

### The consequence nobody had drawn

**If their lines are good, the master line already exists.** «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز» is in market and working.

**The same rule that protects عيش جو المغامرة protects it: replacing a working line to justify a deliverable destroys equity TSA was hired to build.** So two of the five brands are already solved, and the honest deliverable is **three lines and a giveaway, not five and a giveaway.** Smaller bill of work, better answer, and it only appeared once the register question was settled.

### The rewrite

Built on their patterns rather than against them. **مع + أحلى** is their structure, so BeBo gets «مع بيبو، البيت أحلى» and POLEKA gets «مع بوليكا، اللعب أحلى». AlRawy borrows their own trust word: **«الراوي.. طعم تثق فيه»** echoes «جودة نثق بها», which is the client's own language for exactly what AlRawy is for.

**All marked drafts for Aly's ear.** Ten lines passed every structural test yesterday and failed the only test that counts.

## Session 4 — 4:10 AM: BeBo has a line, and Aly out-picked me

**«بيبو معاك كل يوم».** He took the third alternate, not the one I led with.

### He was right, and the reason is structural rather than taste

I recommended **«مع بيبو، الفسحة أحلى»** because الفسحة is the child's own word and it ties to the one occasion the client named. **That is exactly what is wrong with it.**

**It locks the brand to school.** BeBo sells all year, and school stops for three months. **An occasion-bound line goes quiet with the occasion**, which makes it a campaign line wearing a brand line's clothes. §4's own test 3 says a brand line has to hold across every context, and I recommended one that does not.

**«كل يوم» does the opposite: it claims the habit.** For an everyday affordable drink that is the whole ask, and it is the sort of claim a brand line can hold for years without ever needing a document behind it.

**The rest of it checks out too:** «معاك» is second person singular and direct, so there is no parent in between, which was the correction being made an hour earlier. It names no format, so it survives the back-of-pack answer either way. It works across all five SKUs. Brand name inside the line, in their register, no cleverness. And three beats — بي-بو · مَ-عاك · كل-يوم — so a child can repeat it.

### One territory line that fell out of it

**BeBo owns «معاك». POLEKA owns «صاحبك».** Accompaniment against companionship, and they sit close enough that if both ranges use both words the two brands blur. **POLEKA's entire mechanic is the character as a friend**, so «صاحبك» came out of BeBo's words-in list in the same pass.

### Where the deliverable stands

**Three of five settled.** The master keeps «طعم أحلى مع أليكس فودز» and 2MAN keeps «عيش جو المغامرة» — both theirs, both in market, both working, and neither touched. BeBo now has a line. **Outstanding: AlRawy, POLEKA, and the giveaway song.**

## Session 16 — 6:37 PM: The drift checker, and why the PDF check had to be line-based

Continuing work started in another session: *"The PDF check isn't marker-aware like the others. Making it line-based so history is allowed."*

**That checker is not in this repo.** Searched all nine remote branches — no check script exists on any of them, so it is uncommitted in that session's own container and unreachable from here. Built it here instead, with the PDF check line-based and marker-aware from the start.

### Why line-based is the whole point

This vault keeps its own history in place deliberately. `Alex Foods Brands.md` line 17 reads: *"`Alex Foods - Design System v1.1.pdf` … *(v1.0 was superseded the same day and removed)*"* — **one line naming both a file that exists and a file that does not**, and the sentence is correct and permanent.

A file-based check has exactly two options on that note, and both are wrong: fail it forever, or exempt the whole file and go blind to real drift three lines below. **Line-based plus a marker vocabulary is the only thing that separates a record from a rot.**

Three categories, not two — the second one only showed up once the check ran against real content:

| Category | Example in the vault | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **History** | "v1.0 was superseded and removed" | exempt |
| **Planned** | `Brand Book Spec` § 3: **Delivered as** `Alex Foods — Brand Foundation v1.0.pdf` | exempt — the book is not built yet |
| **Drift** | a note naming a file that should be there and is not | **flag** |

### What it found on its first real run

Seven PDF findings, and they sorted into three genuinely different outcomes — which is the useful part, because the temptation was to widen the exemptions until it went quiet.

1. **Five in the daily notes — false positives, fixed in the checker.** A log is history by construction: *"Built and shipped `Design System v1.0.pdf`"* was true on 18 September and stays true as a sentence about that day. **The filename is the marker**, so daily notes are exempt from the PDF check as a class rather than needing a word added to every line. They stay in all five other checks.
2. **One in `Active Priorities` — a gap in the vocabulary, fixed in the checker.** The line said *"two notes pointed at a **deleted** `Design System v1.0.pdf`"*. "Deleted" is plainly a history marker and was missing from the list. Added, with "never committed" and "not in the vault".
3. **One real defect, fixed in the note.** `Alex Foods Brands` § "The Canva mockup" opens *"A file named `bebo_mucup.pdf` arrived 18 September"* and reads as though the file is in the folder. **It is not, deliberately** — a 9.9 MB Canva deck whose whole value is two images that were extracted and kept, recorded as a decision on 18 Sep and never carried into the note itself. A future session would have gone looking for it. The line now says it is not in the vault and why.

### The self-test, because a check that only says "clean" is worthless

Wrote a note with six deliberate defects and ran it:

- `Ghost Report v9.9.pdf` with no marker → **flagged**
- **the same filename** on the next line with "was superseded and removed" → **passed**

## Session 17 — 6:55 PM: Completing the vault doctor — the PDF check

Aly: *"The other session I was building vault doctor, can u complete that."* It is `05 - Resources/vault-check/`, and it arrived on `main` in the merge. The piece left open was the one he named earlier: **the PDF check isn't marker-aware like the others.**

### Why the PDF check is the one that matters

`check.py` walked every `.md` and `.py` and passed clean. **It could not see a single PDF** — and the shipped PDF is the thing that actually reaches the client. That is precisely how the BeBo reversal survived to the printed page Aly was holding when he asked the question this tool exists to answer.

### Building it

No poppler in this environment and no PDF library, so `pdftext.py` decodes the files directly: FlateDecode content streams, ToUnicode CMaps, the text operators. Same approach `design-system/pdf2svg.py` already uses for vector artwork.

**It rebuilds lines, and that is the entire point.** A PDF is a bag of positioned glyphs. Every other check here is line-based because **a line is the unit that can carry a history marker**, so the glyphs get regrouped by text-matrix y-position before the same rules apply. Match a document as one blob and you are back to pass-everything-or-fail-everything, and an audit-trail page fails the build forever.

Two dead ends worth not repeating, both banked in the README:

1. **Chromium emits `<0031> Tj`** — hex strings, not `(text) Tj`. My first parser handled only literal strings, extracted **zero lines**, and looked entirely reasonable doing it.
2. **`1 0 0 -1 x y Tm` means y-down**, so pages read in *ascending* y. Sorting descending silently reverses every page.

**Arabic turned out to be load-bearing rather than an edge case.** The brand book is 1,106 lines and almost all Arabic, which comes out of a PDF in visual order as presentation-form glyphs — so a pattern typed the way a human writes it never matches. `variants()` folds presentation forms with NFKC and reverses to restore logical order; patterns match against raw, folded and reversed.

### Proving it bites, which is the only thing that makes a green check mean anything

The real proof case was already sitting in the vault. TSA's own `TSA Brand Lock Reference v3.1.pdf` page 1 says: *"›Retired: 'The Only Agency In Alex That Guarantees Your Results.'"* — a retired claim, stated inside a shipped PDF, correctly marked.

- **In the PDF, on a line marked `Retired:` → passes.**
- **The same claim unmarked in a note → fails**, naming file, line, what is true now, and where it was decided.

Same claim, two places, opposite verdicts. **That is what "marker-aware like the others" means**, demonstrated rather than asserted. A temporary rule against a real Arabic line in the book also confirmed it names the right **page number** — which is what you need when you are holding the printout.
